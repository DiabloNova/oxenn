# Architecture Gap ARCH-001 — Independent OXENN service catalog and entitlements are not represented as purchasable service SKUs

**Type:** New architecture-driven gap discovered during the 2026-11-07 architecture reconciliation  
**Priority:** P1 — commercial model and entitlement integrity  
**Disposition:** ARCHITECTURE GAP — implementation required before relying on independent service sales  
**Evidence baseline:** `DiabloNova/oxenn@7bd2b6ba04e18abbcaf772fac63f29c465a45a42`  
**Architecture baseline:** `docs/architecture/OXENN_PRODUCT_ARCHITECTURE.md`

## 1. Problem and evidence

The existing dashboard service catalog presents ten capability cards, including Technical SEO, Schema & Metadata, AI Visibility Audits, AI Playground, Content Studio, Content Ingestion, Competitor Radar, Citation Explorer, Live Knowledge Graph, and LLM Response Share & Bias.

The prior code review identified these gaps:

- `src/services/dashboard-services/index.ts` assigns a `pricingTier` to individual cards, but access is primarily derived from the workspace-wide plan rather than separately purchased service entitlements.
- `getWorkspacePlan` uses a process-local `workspacePlans` map, while `SubscriptionService` reads subscription data from PostgreSQL. The two sources are not a single consistent source of truth.
- `getWorkspaceUsage` returns hard-coded sample usage rather than durable, tenant-scoped consumption.
- SEO, AEO, and GEO are not modeled as independently purchasable service SKUs. GEO is not a separate SKU in the current catalog.
- A card being visible or marked with a pricing tier does not prove that a customer has purchased that service or is entitled to execute it.

This gap is separate from the distinction between a customer's product being assessed and an OXENN service that the customer purchases.

## 2. Architecture mapping

- Cross-cutting Service Catalog and Commercial Packaging.
- Workspace, Organization, Billing, and server-side Entitlements.
- Digital Product Assessment (SEO, AEO, GEO).
- Planning & Execution Engine for quota-controlled operations.
- PostgreSQL persistence and Contract & Provenance Gates.

## 3. Root cause

The current model appears to conflate capability catalog presentation, workspace plan tiers, service authorization, and usage accounting. The architecture now requires these concerns to be separate and versioned.

## 4. Recommended remediation

Introduce a canonical commercial model with explicit contracts for:

1. **Catalog item / SKU:** stable identifier, version, service family, capabilities, lifecycle, price-plan association, and optional bundle membership.
2. **Subscription item / purchase:** customer/workspace, SKU and version, activation interval, quantity, status, and billing reference.
3. **Entitlement:** server-evaluated permission to invoke a service capability, scoped to workspace/organization and purchased SKU.
4. **Quota policy:** versioned limits and units (for example crawl pages, prompts, models, products, markets, or seats).
5. **Usage reservation and consumption:** durable, atomic accounting tied to an operation, with idempotency, rollback/release on failed work, and period boundaries.
6. **Bundle composition:** optional bundle expansion into service entitlements; never require the customer to buy a bundle to access a standalone SEO, AEO, or GEO service.

Keep the customer product profile and the OXENN service subscription as distinct domain records. Do not encode a customer's assessed product as a billable OXENN SKU.

Move plan, entitlement, and usage reads to one durable PostgreSQL-backed source of truth. Remove process-local plan state and sample usage from production paths. The dashboard should render catalog and entitlement state from server-authoritative data; hiding a button is not an authorization check.

Do not assume a payment gateway or automatic billing flow as part of this issue. Payment provider integration requires a separate provider and operational decision. Until then, purchased/active status must be driven only by an authorized, auditable administrative or billing event.

## 6. Risks and rejected approaches

- A single workspace tier cannot faithfully express independent SEO/AEO/GEO purchases.
- A UI-only service switch can be bypassed by direct API calls.
- A process-local map loses state across restarts and serverless instances.
- Sample usage can mislead customers and make quota enforcement ineffective.
- A bundle must not silently grant capabilities outside its explicit composition.
- Do not add a payment provider or change production billing in this documentation phase.

## 7. Verification plan

- Unit tests for catalog item versioning, bundle composition, and entitlement decisions.
- PostgreSQL integration tests proving one workspace can purchase SEO without AEO/GEO and another can purchase any supported combination.
- Tenant isolation tests for subscription, entitlement, and usage records.
- Concurrent usage-reservation tests proving quotas cannot be exceeded through parallel requests.
- Failure-path tests proving reservations are released or reconciled idempotently.
- Restart/read-after-write tests proving no process-local state is required.
- API tests proving direct requests are rejected when the service entitlement is absent.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

- SEO, AEO, and GEO can be purchased independently.
- Bundles are optional and have explicit, versioned composition.
- Service entitlement is checked server-side for every protected operation.
- Subscription and usage state are durable and tenant-scoped.
- No production path relies on in-memory workspace plans or sample usage.
- Customer product records are distinct from OXENN service catalog and subscription records.
- Tests cover independent purchases, bundle composition, authorization, quotas, concurrency, and persistence.
