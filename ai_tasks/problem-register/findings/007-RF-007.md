# 007 — RF-007: Root product specification does not define the Digital Twin domain explicitly

## Source
- Finding ID: `RF-007`
- Original audit: `ai_tasks/root-files-audit.md`
- Status in source audit: confirmed product-documentation gap; the target architecture must govern the final scope

## Problem
The root `SPEC.md` and `README.md` describe the product around SEO/AI visibility, digital assets, collection, analysis, intelligence, and user action, but do not explicitly define the Digital Twin concept previously described for Oxenn. Missing elements include the representation of real customer data versus a target state, measurement of the gap, evolution toward the target, execution of an approved plan, and a user–AI conversation about that execution.

## Evidence
- `SPEC.md:56-99` describes SEO/AI visibility and the core lifecycle but contains no explicit Digital Twin domain.
- The complete `SPEC.md` did not define the Digital Twin lifecycle.
- `README.md:3-7` positions the product mainly as AI visibility/AEO with SEO-oriented users and reporting.

## Affected files and paths
- `SPEC.md`
- `README.md`
- The authoritative product architecture and Roadmap documents.

## Impact
Without an explicit product contract, product requirements and engineering work may be planned against an incomplete or inconsistent understanding of Oxenn. This can fragment assessment, gap analysis, planning, execution, and continuous improvement into unrelated features.

## Recommended solution
1. Treat the approved current product architecture and Roadmap as the decision point; do not restore older Digital Twin terminology automatically.
2. Confirm the intended product model: customer/product profile, observed real-world state with evidence and freshness, target state, measurable gaps, proposed scenarios, customer-approved plan, execution records, and measured outcomes.
3. If the Digital Twin remains a first-class capability, define its entities, evidence requirements, gap semantics, target-state versioning, approval boundaries, and lifecycle in the authoritative architecture.
4. Map the Digital Twin lifecycle to the existing assessment and improvement domains instead of creating a parallel product subsystem.
5. Update `SPEC.md` and `README.md` only after the product-owner decision, with clear distinctions between implemented, planned, and target-architecture capabilities.
6. If the concept has been intentionally reframed, record the decision and mark older DBT/DTGA references as superseded rather than silently leaving conflicting specifications.

## Architecture alignment
This finding is about product contract consistency. It must align with the current Oxenn product architecture: evidence-backed assessment, product value and competitiveness, simulation/ROI, planning and execution with human approval, and continuous improvement. It must not introduce a generic, disconnected digital-twin platform.

## Verification criteria
- One authoritative product document defines whether Digital Twin is in scope.
- Real state, target state, gap calculation, evidence provenance, and approved execution have unambiguous contracts if retained.
- Product documentation does not claim target capabilities are already implemented.
- Older terminology is explicitly reconciled.
