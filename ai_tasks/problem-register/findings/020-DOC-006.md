# 20 — DOC-006: Current Roadmap does not define the Digital Twin concept as an explicit product contract

## Source
- Finding ID: `DOC-006`
- Original audit: `ai_tasks/docs-folder-audit.md`
- Status: as stated in the source audit; implementation claims remain subject to source verification

## Problem
Earlier information-architecture and design documents describe a Digital Benchmark Twin and Digital Twin Gap Analyzer, including real-world versus twin comparison, gap analysis, sources, planning, execution, and AI discussion. The current Roadmap contains adjacent assessment, competitiveness, human-approved planning, simulation/ROI, and continuous-outcome capabilities, but does not explicitly define the canonical twin model, target-state evolution, gap semantics, or user–AI plan dialogue. It is unclear whether this is an intentional product reframing or an undocumented omission.

## Evidence
- The October 29 site-map/IA audit describes DBT/DTGA and the twin lifecycle.
- Phase 02 Creative Direction uses “Oxenn’s Living Digital Twin”.
- Current Roadmap RM-02, RM-03, RM-04, RM-08, and RM-10 cover adjacent capabilities without a canonical Digital Twin contract.

## Affected files and paths
- `docs/Roadmap/INDEX.md`
- `docs/architecture/OXENN_PRODUCT_ARCHITECTURE.md`
- Earlier IA/design proposals and product specification.

## Impact
The product may be implemented or planned against inconsistent models. Engineering may either omit an intended product capability or create a disconnected Digital Twin subsystem that duplicates assessment and planning domains.

## Recommended solution
1. Obtain an explicit product-owner decision on whether Digital Twin and the user–AI planning dialogue remain first-class product capabilities.
2. If retained, define real-state evidence, target-state versioning, gap metrics, uncertainty, freshness, plan scenarios, human approvals, execution records, and measured outcomes.
3. Map these concepts into the existing Product Intelligence Profile, assessment, simulation/ROI, Planning & Execution Engine, and Continuous Improvement Loop domains; do not create a parallel platform.
4. Define staged acceptance criteria and identify which elements are MVP, later release, or target architecture only.
5. If the product has been intentionally reframed, record the decision and mark older DBT/DTGA proposals as superseded.
6. Update the Roadmap and authoritative product documents only after the decision; do not infer scope from old design material.

## Architecture alignment
The approved architecture already contains assessment, evidence, simulation, planning/execution with human approval, and continuous improvement. The twin concept must be mapped to these domains if approved, not bolted on as an independent subsystem.

## Verification criteria
A documented product decision resolves the terminology/scope; current architecture and Roadmap agree; real/target state and gap contracts are explicit if retained.
